## Where the deck qualifies or corrects the textbook
| Textbook says | What the deck says | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| A *kos* is a "unit of area" (p. 73) | A traditional unit of **distance**, about 2–4 km | Standard references (the Mughal kos was about 4.2 km) |
| India has "more than 19500 spoken dialects" (2011) | 19,569 **raw mother-tongue names**, rationalised into 1,369 mother tongues and grouped into 121 languages | Census 2011, Language Paper 1 of 2018 |
| Racial groups "according to the 1931 census" (p. 74) | Identified as B.S. Guha's colonial-era anthropometric typology; modern genetics shows mixed ancestry, not discrete races | Guha 1935; Reich et al. 2009; Narasimhan et al. 2019; AAPA 2019 |
| Festivals "follow the lunar calendar"; Makar Sankranti on 14 Jan (p. 76) | Makar Sankranti is a **solar** festival, which is why its date is fixed | Standard calendar references |
| "Majority" of citizens speak Hindi; Indians "typically" speak more than two languages | Hindi was the mother tongue of 43.63%; about 26% were bilingual (Census 2011) | Census 2011 language tables |
| Immigration "endangers national security" (p. 77) | Presented as one side of a contested debate, not as a fact | — |
| Cultural appropriation examples: fast food, McDonald's | Kept in the notes, with the usual academic definition and an Indian example (Prada and Kolhapuri chappals, 2025) | News reports, June–July 2025 |

## Design choices
- **No map of India.** Maps on Wikimedia Commons do not reliably show India's official boundaries. Using one in an Indian college presentation could cause problems, so the deck uses a native chart and diagrams instead.
- **Font sizes.** Body text is mostly 15–21 pt rather than the requested 24–32 pt. At 24 pt or more, the prescribed content would not fit in 10 slides. The fuller explanation is in the speaker notes.
